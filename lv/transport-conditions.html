Transport conditions

Between the client (the sender) and the transporter (the carrier) for every car transport booked through Car2Door. Car2Door (StepsNest B.V.) books the transport as the transporter's agent and is not a party to it.
Last updated: 9 November 2026

  1. The contract. The transport contract starts when the transporter accepts the booking. Its details are the booking's, as the Car2Door apps show them: the car, the addresses, the dates, the price and the parties.
  2. The CMR. This carriage is subject, notwithstanding any clause to the contrary, to the Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road (CMR). Nothing here lowers the transporter's liability under it.
  3. Open vehicle. The car is carried on an open vehicle; the parties agree this expressly (CMR Article 17(4)(a)), and the consignment note says so.
  4. Loading. The transporter's driver loads and unloads the car (driven, rolled or winched on and off).
  5. The consignment note. An electronic consignment note (e-CMR) in the Car2Door apps, under the e-CMR Protocol of 2008. Both parties agree to its electronic signatures: the driver and whoever hands over sign at pickup, the recipient at delivery. Every version is kept. For a pickup in Belgium the same note is printed and signed on paper.
  6. The driver and the vehicle. The driver and plates named in the app 24 hours before pickup carry the car. No subcontracting and no reloading onto another vehicle.
  7. Pickup. The client's contact gives the pickup code and may refuse to hand over if the driver or the plates don't match. The driver checks that the VIN on the car is the one booked, takes the four corner photos and close-ups of any damage, and both sign. A car that is not as booked may be refused; that counts as the client's no-show.
  8. The car's papers. The registration paper's vehicle part (Part I) and the keys go with the car, as ticked on the note. The ownership part (Part II) never travels in the car.
  9. On the road. The car is locked, its keys never left in it, and overnight it stands only in a guarded or closed place. The transporter follows instructions only from the client, given in the app (CMR Article 12).
  10. Waiting. The driver waits at least 60 minutes at pickup and at delivery after calling and messaging the contact.
  11. Delivery. Only to the named recipient, against the delivery code. The driver takes the same four photos; the recipient signs and writes any visible damage on the delivery report. If nobody is there, the transporter keeps the car safe and a new delivery is agreed in the trip chat.
  12. Dates. Pickup inside the window, delivery by the deliver-by date the apps show.
  13. Liability. The transporter is liable for loss, damage and delay under the CMR, limited to 8.33 SDR per kilogram of gross weight (CMR Article 23), more only for wilful misconduct. For a car worth more than €15,000 the value written on the consignment note is declared under CMR Article 24, and the limit is that value; the price includes any surcharge for it.
  14. Claims. Visible damage at delivery on the delivery report; other damage in writing within 7 days of delivery, Sundays and public holidays not counted; delay within 21 days; a court claim within one year, three for wilful misconduct (CMR Articles 30 and 32). A report through the Car2Door app is written notice to the transporter.
  15. Payment. The client pays the price to Car2Door as the transporter's collection agent, which counts as payment to the transporter. The transporter never asks the client for more.
  16. Cancellation. As the client terms say (section 13 and 14), for both parties.
  17. Law and courts. Dutch law, together with the CMR. Courts as CMR Article 31 allows: the country where the defendant is established, or where the car was taken over or was to be delivered.
